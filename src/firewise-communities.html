Fire adapted communities

Firewise works at the neighborhood scale

One prepared house on a street of unprepared ones is still in trouble. Embers do not respect lot lines, and a house that ignites becomes the next source of heat and embers for its neighbors. Organizing the block is the point.

What Firewise USA® actually is

Firewise USA® is a national recognition program run by the National Fire Protection Association. It gives a neighborhood a structure: get your risk assessed by people who know fire, write down what you are going to do about it, do some of it every year, and stay in touch with your fire department while you do.

It is not a grant program and it is not an inspection that can fail you. It is a framework plus a national roster — and in practice, a reason for a set of neighbors to actually meet about this instead of meaning to.

Recognition sometimes matters beyond the fire itself: some insurers ask about community wildfire mitigation, and some grant programs favor organized neighborhoods. Ask your own carrier rather than assuming — it varies a great deal between companies.

The steps to recognition

Most Travis County neighborhoods get through this in a few months of part-time volunteer work. The Alliance can walk a new group through it.

  1. Define the area and form a committee

    A Firewise site is a set of contiguous dwellings — typically 8 to 2,500 — with a board or committee of residents willing to run it. An HOA works, and so does an informal group on a few streets.

  2. Get a written wildfire risk assessment

    From your state forestry agency or your local fire department — here, Texas A&M Forest Service, the Austin Fire Department, or your Emergency Services District. It identifies the hazards specific to your streets rather than to the region.

  3. Write a three-year action plan

    Built from the assessment: what gets addressed, in what order, by whom. Plain and short beats comprehensive and unread.

  4. Invest the annual minimum

    At least the equivalent of one volunteer hour per dwelling unit per year. Residents' own labor on their own property counts, as do contractor costs and donated equipment — which is why most neighborhoods clear this bar easily once they start tracking it.

  5. Hold an outreach event each year

    A chipper day, a work weekend, a walk-through with the fire department, a meeting with a speaker. One per year, and the practical ones tend to draw better than the talks.

  6. Apply, then renew annually

    Submit through the NFPA Firewise USA portal, then file a renewal each year documenting that year's investment and event. The renewal is what keeps a site active.

Know who covers you

Travis County is not served by one fire department, and the answer changes who you ask for an assessment.

Inside the City of Austin

The Austin Fire Department covers wildfire response and works with Firewise neighborhoods across the city, and the city's WUI code governs construction in mapped interface areas.

Elsewhere in Travis County

Most of the unincorporated county and several smaller cities are served by Emergency Services Districts — separate taxing districts running their own departments. Find the ESD covering your address; they are your first call for an assessment and your partner in a Firewise application. Travis County emergency services

What keeps a site alive after year one

Recognition is the easy part. Neighborhoods lose it the same few ways.

Spread the work

A committee of one is a committee that ends when that person moves. Name a backup for every role from the beginning.

Log hours as they happen

Reconstructing a year of volunteer time in the week the renewal is due is miserable. A shared sheet takes seconds per entry.

Make the annual event useful

People come back for a chipper truck, a dumpster, or help on their own property. Attendance is the health signal to watch.

Bring new residents in

Someone who bought last spring does not know the neighborhood does this. A welcome note with the action plan attached costs nothing.

Starting one near you

Tell us where you are and what stage you are at. If there is already a group nearby, we will point you at them rather than have you start from scratch.
