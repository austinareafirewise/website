                This is where everything a visitor reads on the website comes from:
                the meeting notice at the top of the home page, and the news articles.
            


            
The two kinds

            
    
                
  • Meeting notice — a short band across the top of the home
                    page, above the big headline. For a meeting, a chipper day, a seminar:
                    something happening on a date. Keep it to a few sentences.
    • 
                
  • News article — a full page of its own on the News page,
                    with room for pictures. Tick Show the title in the News section on the
                    home page and the headline also appears on the home page.
    • 
            


            
Finding something in the list

            

                The list is in two parts: Meeting notices first, then
                News articles. Within each, items are grouped by what they are
                doing right now — On the site now, Scheduled, Drafts,
                Ended — so something you cannot find on the site is usually a draft or
                an item whose stop date has passed, rather than an item that vanished.
            


            
Draft, Preview, Publish

            
    
                
  1. Save as draft keeps your work. Visitors never see a draft.
                    A draft can be unfinished — no title yet, no dates yet, nothing but a
                    picture you wanted to keep. Save it and come back.
    2. 
                
  2. Preview opens the real page with your item on it — the actual
                    page, with your wording, your picture and your layout. It shows the item
                    whatever its dates say, so it is not a check that the item will be live;
                    it is a check of how it reads.
    3. 
                
  3. Publish puts it on the site. This is the step that insists on
                    a title, some text and both dates.
    4. 
            

            

                Take off the site turns a published item back into a draft —
                it is not deleted. Delete removes it for good, and asks first.
                Duplicate makes a copy to start the next one from, which is the
                easy way to do a monthly meeting notice.
            


            
Show from, Show until

            

                On a meeting notice, "Show from" does two jobs: it is when the
                notice starts appearing, and it is the date and time printed on the notice for
                visitors to read. Set it to when the meeting is, not to when you happen to be
                writing it, or the notice will announce the wrong day.
            

            

                Both are required to publish, and both are Central time. The item appears on the
                site at the first time and disappears at the second, on its own — nobody has to
                remember to take it down. A published item whose start time has not arrived shows
                in the list as Scheduled.
            

            

                Allow up to a minute for a change to reach the public site.
            


            
When is it?

            

                A meeting notice can carry the date and time of the thing itself — the meeting,
                the chipper day, the seminar — with an optional end time. That is what gets printed
                on the notice for visitors to read.
            

            

                It is separate from Show from and Show until,
                which only decide when the notice is on the website. They pull in opposite
                directions: you want the notice up weeks before, and you want it to say the real
                date of the meeting. One field could not do both.
            

            

                It is optional, because not every notice is an event — "chipper day moved",
                "Walnut Creek is recognized". A notice without it simply prints no time. Publishing
                a meeting or an event with no time asks you once, in case it was an oversight.
            


            
What kind of notice

            

                A meeting notice carries a small label and a colored edge saying what it is:
                Meeting notice, Event, Please read
                or Good news. You choose the kind; the site chooses the color.
                That is deliberate — it keeps the colors meaning the same thing everywhere, and
                it means a notice can never come out in a color nobody can read.
            

            

                Use Please read sparingly. It is the loudest one, and it stops
                being loud if every notice uses it.
            


            
Pictures

            

                News articles take up to three. A meeting notice takes one, drawn small beside the
                words — a notice sits above the headline on the home page, so a large picture there
                pushes the whole page down. Drop one on the dotted box, drag the frame to
                choose what shows, and say what is in the picture — that sentence is what someone
                using a screen reader hears instead of seeing it, so it is required.
            

            

                Each picture sits either across the whole width or
                beside the text, left or right, with the writing flowing round
                it. Pictures are spread down the article in the order you add them: the first
                above the first paragraph, the next a couple of paragraphs below. You can change
                where one sits at any time from its card — no need to upload it again. On a phone
                every picture goes full width, because a picture and two words beside it is worse
                than either alone.
            

            

                If a photo comes out on its side, use Turn left or Turn
                right above the frame — each press is a quarter turn, and the frame goes
                back to the middle afterwards. A picture already on the article can be turned too:
                use Rotate / re-crop on its card and it is replaced where it
                stands, keeping its description, credit and position.
            

            

                Only the cropped part is kept, and it is re-made on your own computer before it is
                sent, which strips the location and camera information phones hide inside photos.
            


            
The colors on the News page

            

                Each article gets a colored edge, taken from the Alliance's own colors. It is
                chosen from the article itself, so it never changes once published and there is
                nothing for you to pick — it exists only so a page of articles does not look like
                one gray block. It carries no meaning, unlike the notice kinds above.
            


            
Link to another website

            

                Optional. Adds a button under the text — registration, an agency notice, a map.
                It must start with https://, and it opens in a new tab so the visitor
                still has our site behind them.
            


            
Markdown

            

                Leave the Markdown box unticked and your text appears exactly as you typed it,
                with a new paragraph wherever you left a blank line. That is enough for most
                notices. Tick it if you want headings, bold or bullet points — the
                What is Markdown? button beside the box shows how.
            

        


        

            

                The top of the home page: the small line above the headline, the headline, the
                paragraph under it, and a picture beside them. Nothing else on the home page is
                edited here.
            


            
What is and is not on this tab

            

                The meeting notices, the news titles and the sign-up form each come from their own
                section. The four buttons under the banner — Prepare your home, Start a Firewise
                community, Sign up for alerts, Get Alliance updates — are how visitors get around
                the site rather than wording, so they are deliberately out of reach here and
                cannot be moved or lost by an edit.
            


            
The line above the headline

            

                Set in small capitals with wide letter-spacing whatever you type, so it holds a few
                words and not a sentence — where the Alliance works, or a season. Leaving it empty
                takes the line away rather than leaving a gap.
            


            
The headline

            

                A line break you type becomes a line break on the page, which is how the two short
                lines up there are made. It is set very large, so a sentence that read well in the
                box can run to three lines on the site. Preview before you publish.
            


            
The picture

            

                Decoration, and treated as such: one at a time, beside the words on a computer, and
                not shown at all on a phone rather than pushing the headline down a small screen.
                Never put words you need read inside it. It is cropped and described the same way a
                news picture is, and the description is required — that is what somebody using a
                screen reader hears instead of seeing it.
            


            
Today’s fire danger

            

                With no picture there, the site draws the fire danger sign instead — the five-level
                gauge with the arrow, the one on the signs outside the fire stations. Nobody sets
                it. The rating comes from Texas A&M Forest Service, who run the National Fire
                Danger Rating System for Texas, and the site reads it every few hours.
            

            

                Five weather stations in and around Travis County are read and
                the highest of them is shown. They disagree on an ordinary day,
                and a neighbor told High on a Moderate day has lost nothing. Unlike a picture the
                sign stays on a phone: a picture is decoration and can be dropped on a small
                screen, a rating somebody might act on cannot.
            

            

                A picture always wins. Add one and the sign steps aside, without your having to
                turn anything off. The checkbox is for the other case — no picture, and you would
                rather the space were empty than have the sign there.
            

            

                If the rating cannot be read, or the newest one is more than a day and a half old,
                the sign disappears rather than showing yesterday’s danger under
                today’s date. An empty space beside the headline means exactly that, and
                nothing is wrong with the page.
            


            
Saving

            

                Save and publish does both in one press. There is no draft here and
                no second person to approve it: the banner is one record, and it is either the
                wording on the site or it is not. Allow up to a minute, then reload the site. There
                is nothing to undo — only the next version.
            

        


        

            

                Everyone who asked to hear from us: from the sign-up form on the home page, or
                added here by hand.
            


            
How somebody joins

            

                Filling in the form is not enough on its own. We send one message asking them to
                confirm, and nothing else is ever sent until they do. That is a legal requirement
                for email and for texts, and it is also what keeps our messages out of spam
                folders. Somebody who signed up but has not confirmed shows as
                Pending; if they never confirm, their record is deleted
                automatically after a week.
            


            
What the statuses mean

            
    
                
  • Confirmed — they answered the confirmation. They receive
                    messages.
    • 
                
  • Pending — signed up, has not confirmed yet. They receive
                    nothing. Use Resend if they say it never arrived, and tell
                    them to look in their spam folder.
    • 
                
  • Unsubscribed — they asked to stop, or replied STOP to a text.
                    Their record is kept on purpose, so that a later form submission cannot quietly
                    put them back on the list.
    • 
                
  • Bounced — the address does not exist, or rejected us
                    permanently. Nothing more is sent there. Continuing to mail addresses that
                    bounce is what gets a whole domain blocked.
    • 
            


            
Adding someone by hand

            

                Only for somebody who actually asked — a paper sign-up sheet at a meeting, or a
                request in person. The form makes you write down how consent was
                obtained, and that sentence is stored with their record as the evidence.
                They still get the confirmation message; nobody can be marked confirmed by hand.
            

            

                Never add an address from a directory, a neighborhood list, or another
                organization's roster. That is what the law calls an imported list, and one
                complaint from it can cost us the ability to send anything at all.
            


            
Unsubscribe, or delete?

            

                Unsubscribe when they ask to stop hearing from us: messages stop
                immediately and the record stays, so they cannot be re-added by accident.
                Delete only when they ask to be erased entirely — it removes the
                record and the consent evidence with it.
            


            
The other buttons

            

                Search matches name, email, phone and neighborhood.
                Download CSV gives you the whole list as a spreadsheet — it is
                personal information, so keep it off shared drives and delete your copy when you
                are done with it.
            

        


        

            

                Email and text messages to the membership. Nothing here is sent by one person
                alone: somebody writes it, somebody else approves it, and only then can it go.
            


            
The order things happen

            
    
                
  1. Draft — write it. Only you see it. Edit it as often as you
                    like.
    2. 
                
  2. Send me a test — sends the real message to you alone. Do this
                    before every submission. It is the only way to see what it actually looks like
                    on a phone.
    3. 
                
  3. Submit for review — hands it to the rest of the board.
                    From this point the message cannot be edited: whoever approves
                    it has to be reading exactly what will go out. If you need to change something,
                    press Withdraw and it becomes a draft again, with its Edit
                    button back.
    4. 
                
  4. Approve — done by a different board member. You cannot approve
                    your own message; the button is not there for the person who wrote it. Anyone
                    else can Request changes instead, with a note, which sends it
                    back as a draft.
    5. 
                
  5. Send now — the last step, and it asks first.
    6. 
            

            

                A message that has gone out cannot be recalled, corrected or deleted. Read it once
                more before pressing Send.
            


            
Who it goes to

            

                Email subscribers, text subscribers, or
                everyone. People chose which when they signed up, so someone who
                only wanted email never gets a text. Unconfirmed, unsubscribed and bounced people
                are skipped automatically.
            


            
Writing it

            
    
                
  • Name is for your own list. It is never sent.
    • 
                
  • Email subject and Email message — the message
                    accepts Markdown. The unsubscribe link and our postal address are added for
                    you; both are required by law and neither can be removed.
    • 
                
  • Text message — short. "Reply STOP to stop" is added for you.
                    Watch the count shown beside the box: a long text is billed and delivered as
                    several, and links and emoji eat the allowance fast.
    • 
                
  • Send at — leave it blank to send when approved, or set a time.
                    A routine text can only be scheduled between 8am and 9pm Central; outside that the
                    form refuses it. And if the window closes between approval and the message actually
                    going out, the email still goes and the text waits until the morning on its own —
                    nobody has to notice or do anything.
    • 
                
  • Kind of message — routine or emergency. This is the one that
                    decides whether a text can go out at three in the morning.
    • 
            


            
Routine, and emergency

            

                A routine text waits for 8am to 9pm Central. If you schedule one
                outside that window the form refuses it, and if the window closes between approval
                and sending, the send holds until morning by itself.
            

            

                An emergency goes at once, at any hour of any day. That is what it
                is for: an evacuation, a fire closing a road, where the shelter is. The quiet hours
                exist for marketing, and the rules have always treated a message about an active
                threat to health and safety differently.
            

            

                It is not a way to make a message feel important. Marking a meeting reminder an
                emergency tells the phone companies it is something it is not, and that is what
                costs an organization its number — the same mistake as calling a newsletter
                transactional to get it delivered faster. Every emergency records who chose it.
            


            
Afterwards

            

                A sent message stays in the list with the date it went and who approved it.
                Duplicate starts a new draft from it, which is how to do a
                recurring reminder without retyping it.
            

        


        

            

                The neighborhood groups the Alliance works with. This list is also the dropdown
                people choose from on the public sign-up form, so what you write here is read by
                residents.
            


            
Status, and what it changes

            
    
                
  • Prospective — interested, nothing started yet.
    • 
                
  • In progress — working towards recognition.
    • 
                
  • Recognized Firewise USA site — recognized, and current. Put
                    the year in if it is to hand; it is not required.
    • 
                
  • Lapsed — was recognized, the renewal is overdue.
    • 
                
  • Inactive — hides the group from the sign-up form. Use this
                    rather than deleting a group that no longer meets: deleting loses its history.
    • 
            


            
Filling it in

            

                City is Austin, Lago Vista, Jonestown and so on, and it is the one
                field here besides the name that visitors see: the sign-up dropdown reads
                "Jester Estates — Austin". With Area it covers blank on most
                records, the city is often the only thing telling two similar names apart, so fill
                it in.
            

            

                Area it covers should read the way a resident would describe where
                they live — streets, a subdivision, "everything north of the creek" — not a formal
                boundary. That is the line somebody uses to work out whether the group is theirs.
                ZIP codes are comma separated. Households is the
                number the group counts for its Firewise paperwork.
            


            
This group's own board and contacts

            

                Add the people who actually run the group: first name, last name, a role, and a way
                to reach them.
                These are the neighborhood's own officers. They are not the
                Alliance's board of directors, which is a separate list on the
                Alliance board tab — somebody can be on both, but each is entered
                where it belongs.
            

            

                This is the Alliance's own record of who to call, not a public directory — nothing
                in the group record is shown on the website except the group's name and city in the
                sign-up dropdown.
            


            
Importing from a file

            

                Import from a file reads the board's own spreadsheet, saved as CSV
                or tab-separated text with headings in the first row, so forty groups do not have to
                be typed in one at a time. A row with a group name starts a group; a row with no
                group name adds another contact to the group above it, which is how the board's own
                sheet is already laid out.
            

            

                Nothing is sent until you have read the preview and pressed Import.
                Groups already on the site are skipped rather than duplicated, so running the same
                file twice does not double anything, and any row that fails is listed with its
                reason while the rest carry on.
            


            
Renewal due

            

                Firewise USA recognition has to be renewed. Put the date here so the board can see
                what is coming; a group that goes past it should be moved to Lapsed
                until it is renewed.
            


            
Deleting a group

            

                People who chose that group when they signed up are not deleted with it — their
                records are kept and simply lose the group. The dialog tells you how many were
                affected.
            

        


        

            

                The Resources page: links to other organizations' pages, and documents of our own
                for people to download, grouped under a handful of headings.
            


            
A link, or a file

            

                A link sends somebody to another organization's website. Paste the
                address of the page that has the thing on it — not the organization's front page,
                which leaves the reader to go hunting. It has to start with https://.
            

            

                A file is something of ours to download: a PDF, a Word document, a
                spreadsheet, plain text, or an MP4 video.
            


            
Files go up exactly as you give them

            

                Unlike photographs, documents are not re-saved on the way up. That means whatever
                is inside goes up too — the author's name, comments in the margins, tracked changes
                nobody accepted, rows hidden rather than deleted. Open the file, read it as a
                stranger would, and save a clean copy first.
            

            

                A video already on YouTube or Vimeo is better added as a link. It plays better on a
                phone, carries its own captions, and costs the Alliance nothing to serve.
            


            
Categories

            

                The Categories button changes the headings. Renaming one moves
                everything under it with no work. A category cannot be removed while anything is
                still filed in it — move those first. Keep the list short: a page with more headings
                than things under them stops being read.
            


            
The weekly check

            

                Every Sunday morning the site tries each link and records what happened. You do not
                have to do anything for that.
            

            

                A link that fails once is marked here but stays on the website. The
                sites worth linking to often refuse automated requests even when they are working
                perfectly, so one failure proves nothing. Only after three weeks running
                does the site take a link down by itself — and if it starts answering again, it comes
                back on its own.
            

            

                So a warning here is an invitation to check, not an emergency. Open the link yourself.
                If the page has moved, paste the new address. If it is really gone, take ours down too.
            

        


        

            

                Who is on the board of the Alliance, with roles and terms. This is the
                organization's own record.
            


            
This list is public

            

                Everybody on it with the status Current board member is shown on
                board.html, linked from the footer of every page. Name and role,
                and nothing else: the email and phone on the record are how the Alliance reaches
                its own officers and are never published. A past board member is not shown.
            

            

                It follows that a misspelled name or a role typed in a hurry is on the website
                within a minute. Read the row back before you leave it.
            


            
Not the same as a group's officers

            

                A Firewise group has its own chair and contacts. Those belong on that group's
                record under Firewise groups, not here. This tab is the Austin
                Area Firewise Alliance board of directors and nobody else.
            


            
Forgotten password

            

                On the sign-in page, use Forgot your password? under the password
                box. A six-digit code is emailed to the address your account is registered under,
                and you set a new password yourself. It has to be at least twelve characters with
                an upper-case letter, a lower-case letter and a number.
            

            

                The code comes from an Amazon address rather than from us, so look in the spam
                folder if it does not arrive within a minute. If you can no longer get into that
                mailbox at all, nobody on the board can reset it for you — whoever manages the AWS
                account has to do it.
            


            
A board record is not a login

            

                These two things are deliberately separate. Adding somebody here does
                not let them edit the website, and removing them does
                not take that away. Website access is granted and removed
                separately, by whoever manages the AWS account, so that the roster can be kept
                accurate by anyone without accidentally handing out or cutting off access.
            

            

                When somebody leaves the board, do both: set them to Past board
                member here, and ask whoever manages the account to remove their login.
            


            
Current and past

            

                Set Past board member rather than deleting somebody who has served
                — the record of who held which office and when is worth keeping, and a past member
                needs an end date. Delete only a record that should never have existed, such as a
                duplicate.
            


            
The other fields

            

                First name and last name are separate boxes, the
                same as a member and the same as a group's leadership — one person is recorded the
                same way everywhere. A record entered before this change keeps the name it had; open
                it and check the two boxes split it correctly, then save.
                Role is free text — chair, treasurer, secretary, member at large.
                Their Firewise group is optional and only records which
                neighborhood group they belong to, if any; it does not give them anything.
                Email and phone are for the board's own use and are not published.
            

        



        

            

                Worth ten minutes once. Most questions about "why did it do that" are answered by
                knowing these few things.
            


            
Finding your way around

            

                The tabs across the top are the six sections. Opening or adding anything —
                an article, a member, a group, a board member, a message — replaces the list with
                a full page for that one record, and ← Back to the list at the top
                returns you. Nothing opens in a small pop-up box any more, so a long form gets the
                whole window.
            

            

                The buttons stay at the bottom of the screen while you scroll, and anything the
                form needs to tell you appears just above them. You should never have to scroll to
                find out whether something saved.
            


            
Two separate halves

            

                Editing and reading are deliberately kept apart. What you do here goes through a
                sign-in, an API and a database. The public website does none of that — it reads a
                single file of already-published content and some pictures, and nothing else.
            

            

                That is the reason the site stays up. If this admin area is broken, offline, or
                being attacked, visitors see no difference at all: the site keeps serving whatever
                was last published. On a page people may open during an actual fire, having no
                moving parts on the public side is the property that matters most.
            


            
What "published" actually does

            

                Pressing Publish rewrites one file that the public site reads. It contains every
                published item whose stop date has not yet passed — including ones scheduled for
                next month. Your browser shows only the ones whose window includes right now, which
                is why a notice scheduled for next Tuesday appears on Tuesday without anybody doing
                anything. No timer runs anywhere.
            

            

                Because scheduled items sit in that public file early, somebody determined enough
                could read next week's notice a week early. For meetings and chipper days that is
                harmless. It does mean nothing confidential should ever be typed into this
                system — not a member's private circumstances, not an address someone
                wants kept quiet, not anything about an incident. If something must be held back,
                leave it as a draft: drafts are never in the public file.
            


            
The four states an item can be in

            
    
                
  • Draft — saved, never public. Can be previewed.
    • 
                
  • Scheduled — published, start date still to come. It appears on
                    its own.
    • 
                
  • Live — published and inside its window. On the site now.
    • 
                
  • Ended — stop date passed. Hidden from visitors, kept for the
                    record. Delete it when you no longer want it in the list.
    • 
            

            

                Drafts and ended items sit grayed at the bottom of the list. Anything not live is
                labelled as such, on purpose.
            


            
Why a change takes a minute

            

                The published file is cached for about a minute so the site stays fast. A change can
                take that long to show up for visitors, and refreshing your own browser harder will
                not hurry it. Preview does not go through the cache, so it is always current.
            


            
Where each thing appears

            
    
                
  • The burn ban bar — the strip above the header, which the site
                    fills in itself from the Travis County Fire Marshal's page every half hour.
                    Nobody edits it. If the county's page cannot be read, or the last reading is
                    more than six hours old, it falls back to standing wording that is true
                    whatever the county has done.
    • 
                
  • The banner — the line above the headline, the headline
                    itself, the paragraph under it, and a picture beside them. Edited on the

What happens to a picture

            

                Cropping happens on your own computer, before anything is sent. What leaves your
                machine is the cropped result at two sizes; the original file never does. That has a
                side effect worth knowing: re-making the picture strips the hidden
                information phones bury in photos, including GPS coordinates. A board
                member's photo of a chipper day will not publish the location of their driveway.
            


            
Why there is no formatting toolbar

            

                Everything typed here reaches the website as text, never as page code. That is a
                deliberate wall: it means an editor account in the wrong hands still cannot put
                anything harmful into the site. Markdown does not change it — the website reads the
                punctuation itself and builds the headings and lists, and anything it does not
                recognize is shown exactly as typed. It is also why a meeting notice stays plain.
            


            
If something looks wrong

            
    
                
  • A change has not appeared — give it a minute, then check the
                    item is Live and not Draft or Scheduled.
    • 
                
  • An item vanished from the site — its stop date almost certainly
                    passed. It is still in the list, under Ended.
    • 
                
  • Signed in but nothing works — sign out and back in; the
                    sign-in expires after a while.
    • 
                
  • An error you cannot get past — nothing is changed when a save
                    fails, so it is safe to leave it and ask for help.

Things worth knowing

            
    
                
  • Leave a blank line between paragraphs. One line break on its
                    own does nothing — this is the mistake everybody makes first.
    • 
                
  • The # and - must be at the start of the
                    line, with a space after them.
    • 
                
  • Links must start with https://. Anything else is left as plain
                    text.
    • 
                
  • Anything the website does not recognize is shown exactly as you typed it. A
                    stray asterisk is a stray asterisk, not an error.
    • 
                
  • Untick the box and the whole lot appears literally, punctuation and all. If
                    the article is plain sentences, leave it unticked.
    • 
            


            

                Use Preview before publishing. It shows the real page, and it is
                quicker than wondering.
