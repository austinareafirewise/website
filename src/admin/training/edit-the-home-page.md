## The top of the site, and nothing else
- section: home
- target: #f-home-headline
- audio: edit-the-home-page-01-bf1bbc.mp3
- audio-hash: 453c776f

The home banner. This lesson is the band at the very top of the site — the
big headline, the words under it, and the picture beside them. It is the Home
banner tab, and the heading on the screen says Home page banner.

The box outlined now is the headline itself. A line break you type here
becomes a line break on the page, which is how the two short lines up there
are made.

Nothing else on the home page is edited from here. The meeting notices, the
news, the sign-up form and the four buttons all come from somewhere else, and
the buttons are deliberately out of reach: they are how people get around the
site, not wording, and a form is how one goes missing.

## The small line above it
- section: home
- target: #f-home-kicker
- audio: edit-the-home-page-02-bf2eec.mp3
- audio-hash: a94c9820

This one is the small green line above the headline. It reads "Travis County,
Texas" today.

It is set in capitals with wide spacing whatever you type, so it holds a few
words and not a sentence. Where the Alliance works, or a season — not an
announcement.

Leaving it empty takes the line away rather than leaving a gap.

## The paragraph
- section: home
- target: #f-home-lede
- audio: edit-the-home-page-03-720ac0.mp3
- audio-hash: 5bf79368

This is the paragraph under the headline, and for most people it is the only
thing they will read about the Alliance before deciding whether the rest is
for them.

Plain sentences. No headings, no bullets, no Markdown here — this box is words
only.

## A picture beside the words
- section: home
- target: #home-dropzone
- audio: edit-the-home-page-04-b911ca.mp3
- audio-hash: 0e54ac82

Drop a photo on the dotted box and you will crop it the same way you would on
a news article.

It is decoration. It sits beside the headline on a computer and it is not
shown at all on a phone, so nothing you say is ever inside the picture. One at
a time: adding another means removing the one there.

Adding one here also takes the fire danger sign away, which is the next step.
You do not have to turn anything off; a picture the board chose wins.

It still needs a sentence saying what is in it. That is what somebody using a
screen reader hears, and it is required for that reason rather than as
paperwork.

## The fire danger sign
- section: home
- target: #f-home-firedanger
- audio: edit-the-home-page-05-bfdb65.mp3
- audio-hash: 0c336c7e

Leave the picture off and the site draws this instead: the fire danger gauge,
the same five levels as the sign outside a fire station.

Nobody sets it. Texas A&M Forest Service rates the fire danger every day, and
the site reads five weather stations around Travis County and shows the
highest of them. If it cannot read today's rating the sign disappears rather
than leaving yesterday's danger up.

Unlike the picture, it stays on a phone. Decoration can be dropped on a small
screen. Something a neighbor might act on cannot.

This box turns it off, for the case where there is no picture and you would
rather the space were empty.

## Look at it before anybody else does
- section: home
- target: #home-preview
- audio: edit-the-home-page-05-10d6d8.mp3
- audio-hash: f02c9e9b

Preview opens the real home page with your words in it. The headline is very
large, so a sentence that read well in the box can run to three lines up
there.

It saves first, so what you are looking at is what is stored — but the site
itself does not change until you publish.

## Save and publish
- section: home
- target: #home-save
- audio: edit-the-home-page-06-e0d5b4.mp3
- audio-hash: 75ac9737

This saves the page and puts it live in one press. There is no draft here and
no second person to approve it: the home page is one record, and it is either
the wording on the site or it is not.

Allow up to a minute, then reload the site. If something came out wrong, fix
the box and press it again — there is nothing to undo, only the next version.
