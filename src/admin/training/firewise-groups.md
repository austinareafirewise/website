## The groups list is public, in one place
- section: groups
- target: #group-new
- audio: firewise-groups-01-8cceb9.mp3
- audio-hash: 2d720300

Firewise groups. These are the neighborhood groups the Alliance works with —
each one its own organization, with its own people.

This list is also the dropdown residents choose from on the sign-up form, so
the names here are read by the public. Nothing else about a group is shown on
the website: not the area, not who runs it.

One thing to be clear about before anything else. A group's chair and contacts
are kept on that group's record, here. The Alliance's own board of directors is
a different list on a different tab. Two different sets of people, and the
group's are never entered on the Alliance board tab.

## Status decides what happens
- section: groups
- screen: editor
- open: #group-new
- target: #f_status
- audio: firewise-groups-02-f03dc1.mp3
- audio-hash: 762ed99e

This is the group's Status, and it decides more than it looks. The choices are
prospective, in progress, recognized, lapsed, and inactive.

Inactive hides the group from the sign-up form. Use that rather than deleting a
group that has stopped meeting — deleting loses its history, and the people who
chose it keep their records but lose the group.

## City, and the area it covers
- section: groups
- screen: editor
- open: #group-new
- target: #f_city
- audio: firewise-groups-03-16079f.mp3
- audio-hash: 5d72f8a2

The box outlined here is City — Austin, Lago Vista, Jonestown. Besides the
name, it is the only thing on this form a visitor sees: the sign-up dropdown
reads "Jester Estates — Austin". Fill it in. Area it covers is blank on nearly
every group we hold, so the city is usually the only thing telling two similar
names apart.

Just below it is that Area box. It is where you say which part of town the
group is, in the words a resident would use — streets, a subdivision,
"everything north of the creek". Not a formal boundary. Somebody reads that
line to work out whether the group is theirs, so write it for them rather than
for the paperwork.

## Forty groups at once
- section: groups
- target: #group-import-open
- audio: firewise-groups-04-22bfaf.mp3
- audio-hash: 09c15008

"Import from a file", outlined now, reads the board's own spreadsheet rather
than making you type it back in. Save it as CSV or tab-separated text, with the
headings in the first row.

It already understands how that sheet is laid out: a row with a group name
starts a group, and a row with no group name adds another contact to the group
above it.

Nothing is sent until you have read the preview and pressed Import. Groups
already on the site are skipped rather than duplicated, so running the same
file twice does not double anything, and any row it cannot take is listed with
the reason while the rest go in.

## The group's own board
- section: groups
- screen: editor
- open: #group-new
- target: #leaders-list
- audio: firewise-groups-04-5a1868.mp3
- audio-hash: 668eaaea

Further down this same form is the group's own board and contacts — their
chair, their block captains, whoever the Alliance actually calls.

This is the neighborhood's leadership, not the Alliance's. Somebody can be on
both, and plenty are, but each list is entered where it belongs: their officers
here, on their group's record, and Alliance directors on the Alliance board
tab.

Add a row per person, first and last name, with a role and how to reach them.
None of it is shown on the public site — it is the Alliance's own contact
sheet.
