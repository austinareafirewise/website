## The groups list is public, in one place
- section: groups
- target: #group-new
- audio: firewise-groups-and-board-01-681b67.mp3
- audio-hash: 34440c73

These are the neighborhood groups the Alliance works with. This list is also
the dropdown people choose from on the sign-up form, so the names are read by
residents.

Nothing else about a group is shown on the website — not the area, not the
leadership.

## Status decides what happens
- section: groups
- target: #group-list
- audio: firewise-groups-and-board-02-9eb1f5.mp3
- audio-hash: f2ac3372

Prospective, in progress, recognized, lapsed, or inactive.

Inactive hides the group from the sign-up form. Use that rather than deleting a
group that has stopped meeting — deleting loses its history, and the people who
chose it keep their records but lose the group.

## Describe the area as a resident would
- section: groups
- screen: editor
- open: #group-new
- audio: firewise-groups-and-board-03-d1d306.mp3
- audio-hash: 137c405d

Streets, a subdivision, "everything north of the creek". Not a formal boundary.

That line is what somebody uses to work out whether the group is theirs, so
write it for them rather than for the paperwork.

## The board roster is not a set of logins
- section: board
- target: #board-note
- audio: firewise-groups-and-board-04-80ad10.mp3
- audio-hash: 4ea86f30

This is who serves on the board. It is deliberately separate from who can sign
in to this website.

Adding somebody here does not give them access, and removing them does not take
it away. That keeps the roster safe for anyone to correct.

## When somebody leaves the board
- section: board
- target: #person-new
- audio: firewise-groups-and-board-05-f55380.mp3
- audio-hash: aa18e131

Do both things. Set them to past board member here, with an end date.

Then ask whoever manages the AWS account to remove their sign-in. One without
the other leaves either a wrong roster or a login that should be gone.
