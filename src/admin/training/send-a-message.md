## Nothing goes out alone
- section: comms
- target: #comm-list
- audio: send-a-message-01-955707.mp3
- audio-hash: e460ebf9

Send a message to members. This lesson covers writing an email or a text to
the membership, and the two-person rule that governs all of it.

One board member writes a message and a different one approves it. You cannot
approve your own — the button is not there for the person who wrote it.

That is the whole design. A message reaching a few hundred people should have
been read by two.

## Write it
- section: comms
- screen: editor
- open: #comm-new
- target: #f_name
- audio: send-a-message-02-04b162.mp3
- audio-hash: dbe358bc

This is the message form, and the box outlined at the top of it is the
message's name. That name is for your own list here. It is never sent to
anybody.

Below it is "Send to": email subscribers, text subscribers, or everyone.
People chose which when they signed up, so somebody who only wanted email
never gets a text.

## The email
- section: comms
- screen: editor
- open: #comm-new
- target: #f_emailBody
- audio: send-a-message-03-cd9392.mp3
- audio-hash: efe6dfe0

This box, outlined now, is the email message itself. The subject goes in the
box just above it.

You can write plain sentences and that is fine. If you want headings, bold
text or bullet points, it understands Markdown, and the "What is Markdown?"
button beside the label shows exactly what to type.

The unsubscribe link and our postal address are added to every email for you.
Both are required by law and neither can be removed.

## And the text message

- screen: editor
- open: #comm-new
- target: #f_smsBody
- audio: send-a-message-04-11b3cc.mp3

This one is the text message, and it is a different job. A text has to be
short.

"Reply STOP to stop" is added for you, and a long message is billed and
delivered as several. There is no Markdown in a text — it is plain words only.

## Read it before anybody else does
- section: comms
- target: #comm-list
- audio: send-a-message-05-219ef8.mp3
- audio-hash: 711f93e7

Preview, on the message's own row, shows exactly what will go out. Not an
impression of it — the same thing the sender builds, with the real footer, the
unsubscribe link and our postal address at the bottom.

It sends nothing and it needs nothing working. Do this first, every time. One
warning: the links in a preview are signed for you, so do not follow them from
there — they would unsubscribe your own record.

## Send yourself a test
- section: comms
- target: #comm-list
- audio: send-a-message-04-a717f4.mp3
- audio-hash: 5a5f123d

Before submitting anything, send yourself a test. It sends the real message to
you alone.

It is the only way to see what it actually looks like on a phone, and it is
where you will notice the wrong date or the link that does not work.

## Submit, and let somebody else approve
- section: comms
- target: #comm-list
- audio: send-a-message-06-91aaea.mp3
- audio-hash: a05cd44a

Submit for review hands it to the rest of the board.

From that moment the message cannot be edited, and the Edit button goes away.
That is deliberate: whoever approves it has to be reading exactly what will go
out. If you need to change something, press Withdraw and it becomes a draft
again, with Edit back.

Anybody else can approve it, or request changes with a note, which also sends
it back to you as a draft.

## Send
- section: comms
- target: #comm-list
- audio: send-a-message-06-7f8595.mp3
- audio-hash: 9d1e78a4

Send now is the last step and it asks first.

A message that has gone cannot be recalled, corrected or deleted. Read it once
more.

## Routine, and emergency
- section: comms
- screen: editor
- open: #comm-new
- target: #f_urgency
- audio: send-a-message-07-2290fd.mp3
- audio-hash: 8ebfe8fb

This box is "Kind of message", and it decides whether a text can go out in the
middle of the night.

A routine text waits for eight in the morning to nine at night. Schedule one
outside that and the form refuses it.

An emergency goes at once, at any hour — an evacuation, a fire closing a road,
where the shelter is. It is not a way to make a message feel important.
Marking a meeting reminder an emergency tells the phone companies something
untrue, and that is what costs an organization its number.
