## Nothing goes out alone
- section: comms
- target: #comm-list
- audio: send-a-message-01-3f83bc.mp3
- audio-hash: 4415db32

One board member writes a message and a different one approves it. You cannot
approve your own — the button is not there for the person who wrote it.

That is the whole design. A message reaching a few hundred people should have
been read by two.

## Write it
- section: comms
- target: #comm-new
- audio: send-a-message-02-00355d.mp3
- audio-hash: 61b21f3e

The name at the top is for your own list and is never sent.

Choose who it goes to: email subscribers, text subscribers, or everyone. People
chose which when they signed up, so somebody who only wanted email never gets a
text.

## Email and text are different
- section: comms
- screen: editor
- open: #comm-new
- audio: send-a-message-03-6fee0e.mp3
- audio-hash: b058fe3c

The email takes a subject and a body, and the body accepts Markdown. The
unsubscribe link and our postal address are added for you — both are required
by law and neither can be removed.

The text has to be short. "Reply STOP to stop" is added for you, and a long
text is billed and delivered as several.

## Send yourself a test
- section: comms
- target: #comm-list
- audio: send-a-message-04-a717f4.mp3
- audio-hash: 5a5f123d

Before submitting anything, send yourself a test. It sends the real message to
you alone.

It is the only way to see what it actually looks like on a phone, and it is
where you will notice the wrong date or the link that does not work.

## Submit, and let somebody else approve
- section: comms
- target: #comm-list
- audio: send-a-message-05-cc011a.mp3
- audio-hash: a0636ffc

Submit for review hands it to the rest of the board. You can still withdraw it.

Anybody else can approve it, or request changes with a note, which sends it
back to you as a draft.

## Send
- section: comms
- target: #comm-list
- audio: send-a-message-06-7f8595.mp3
- audio-hash: 9d1e78a4

Send now is the last step and it asks first.

A message that has gone cannot be recalled, corrected or deleted. Read it once
more.

## Routine, and emergency
- section: comms
- target: #comm-new
- audio: send-a-message-07-12c3f4.mp3
- audio-hash: f6851a69

A routine text waits for eight in the morning to nine at night. Schedule one
outside that and the form refuses it.

An emergency goes at once, at any hour — an evacuation, a fire closing a road,
where the shelter is. It is not a way to make a message feel important.
Marking a meeting reminder an emergency tells the phone companies something
untrue, and that is what costs an organization its number.
