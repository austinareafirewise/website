## What belongs on the Resources page
- section: resources
- target: #resource-new

Resources. This is a page of things we point people at: guidance from the fire
agencies, the paperwork a neighborhood needs to become a Firewise community,
and documents we have written ourselves.

There are two kinds of entry and you will use both. A link out sends someone to
another organization's website. A file is something of ours that they download.
They look the same to a visitor and they are added the same way here.

What does not belong is anything that tells somebody what to do in an
emergency. That lives on the guidance pages, where we control every word of it,
and it is not editable from this screen on purpose.

## Categories keep it findable
- section: resources
- target: #resource-categories

Every resource sits in a category, and the page groups them under those
headings. We start with five — preparing your home, Firewise communities,
evacuation and alerts, insurance and funding, and policy and advocacy — and
you can rename them or add your own.

Keep the number of categories small. Five headings with a handful of things
under each is a page somebody reads. Twelve headings with one thing under each
is a page they give up on, and it is the commonest way a page like this stops
being useful.

If you find yourself wanting a new category for a single item, it usually
belongs in one you already have.

Policy and advocacy needs one extra care. It holds two different kinds of
thing: rules somebody has to follow, like the city's wildland-urban interface
code, and papers saying what the Alliance thinks the rules ought to be. Those
are not the same, and a reader who mistakes the second for the first has been
misled by us. Whichever one you are adding, say so in the first few words of
the description.

## Adding a link to somebody else's page
- section: resources
- screen: editor
- open: #resource-new
- target: #f_url

For a link out, this is where the address goes. Paste the whole thing,
beginning with https.

Two habits worth having. Link to the page that has the thing on it, not to the
organization's front page — sending somebody to a home page to go hunting is
how a good resource gets ignored. And open the link yourself once before you
publish, to be sure it is the page you meant.

## Say what it is, in your own words
- section: resources
- screen: editor
- target: #f_description

The description. Two or three sentences at most, in plain words.

Do not paste the organization's own blurb. Say why somebody in Travis County
would want this and what they will find when they get there. "A checklist for
the first five feet around the house, with photographs" tells a reader more
than the official title ever will.

## Uploading a document of our own
- section: resources
- screen: editor
- target: #f_file

For something of ours, drop the file here. PDFs, Word documents, spreadsheets,
plain text and video are all accepted, and the file is stored exactly as you
provide it — nothing is re-saved or changed.

That last part matters more than it sounds. Because the file goes up untouched,
whatever is inside it goes up too: the author's name, comments left in the
margins, tracked changes that were never accepted, and rows somebody hid rather
than deleted. Open the document, look at it as a stranger would, and save a
clean copy before you upload.

## Video is different
- section: resources
- screen: editor
- target: #f_file

A word about video specifically. A short video is a very large file compared
with everything else here — it is the one thing on this page that costs the
Alliance real money to serve, and it is slow to load for somebody on a phone
out in the county, which is exactly who we are trying to reach.

If the video is already on YouTube or Vimeo, add it as a link instead of
uploading it. It will play better, it costs us nothing, and it carries its
captions with it. Upload video here only when there is nowhere else for it to
live.

## When it shows, and when it stops
- section: resources
- screen: editor
- target: #f_startsAt

Show from and Show until, the same pair you know from meeting notices. The
resource appears at the start and disappears at the stop, on its own.

Most resources have no end. Leave the stop time empty and it stays until you
take it down. Set one for something that expires — a grant that closes, a
workshop that has a date on it, a form that is replaced every year.

## The weekly check, and what a warning means
- section: resources
- target: #resource-list

Once a week, the site quietly tries every link on this page to see whether it
still works. You do not have to do anything for that to happen.

If one fails, it is marked here for you to look at, and it stays on the website
while it is marked. One failure is usually the other organization having a bad
morning, not a dead link. Only after it has failed three weeks running does the
site take it down by itself, and it says so when it does.

So a warning is an invitation to check, not an emergency. Open the link. If it
has moved, paste the new address. If the organization has taken it down, take
ours down too.

## Draft or published, then Save
- section: resources
- screen: editor
- target: #f_status

This one is different from news and notices, so it is worth a moment. There is
no separate Publish button here. Instead, this box at the top says whether the
resource is on the website: Draft keeps it to yourself, Published puts it on
the Resources page.

Choose, then press Save at the bottom. A published resource is on the website
within a minute — unless you gave it a Show from time, in which case it waits
for that.

And the safety net is the same box. If something is up that should not be,
open it, set it back to Draft, and save. It comes off the website within a
minute, and nothing about it is lost.
