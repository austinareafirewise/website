    Five things that matter most

    Most homes lost to wildfire are not overrun by a wall of flame. They are lit by embers that travel ahead of the fire, land in a gutter or against a fence, and smolder. Everything below targets embers.

    • Clear the first five feet. No mulch, firewood, dead plants, or stored items against the house. This strip does more than any other single thing you can do.
    • Clean gutters, roof, and under decks. Dry leaves and cedar litter are exactly what a landing ember needs.
    • Screen every vent. Embers get inside through attic and crawlspace vents. Metal mesh of 1/8 inch or finer, not fiberglass.
    • Trim branches back from the roof. Ten feet of clearance, and limb up trees so fire on the ground cannot climb into the canopy.
    • Know your way out before you need it. Two routes, a meeting point, and a decision to leave early — especially on a street with one exit.

    What the Alliance does

    Wildfire response belongs to the fire departments. Getting a neighborhood ready before that day is work residents have to do themselves — and that is where the Alliance works.

    Helps neighborhoods organize

    Walking a new group through the Firewise USA steps: the risk assessment, the action plan, the annual event, the renewal.

    Connects neighbors to agencies

    Travis County is served by the Austin Fire Department and by a set of Emergency Services Districts. Which one covers you changes who to ask.

    Presses on evacuation planning

    Many neighborhoods here have a single way out. The Alliance asks agencies the specific questions residents need answered, and shares what comes back.

    Shares what works

    What one neighborhood learns about brush disposal, chipper days, or getting an assessment scheduled should not have to be rediscovered by the next one.

    Get updates

    Preparedness reminders ahead of fire season, meeting notices, Firewise recognition news, and answers when agencies respond to our questions. By email, by text, or both. The list is never shared or sold.

    This is not a way to report a fire or request emergency help. If you see fire or smoke, call 911.
