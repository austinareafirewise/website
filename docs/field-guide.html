Things the site does on its own

The burn ban status reads itself

The home page shows Travis County's current burn ban, taken from the county's own page every half hour. If that page cannot be read, the notice hides itself rather than showing something out of date.

The standing rule is always there

Under the emergency line, a fixed banner says burn rules change with conditions and names the county as the authority. It states the rule rather than today's status, so it stays true whatever has happened — and it is what remains if the live status cannot be read.

Text size control

Three sizes in the header of every page, and it remembers each reader's choice. Built because our audience skews toward older homeowners.

The 911 line comes first

Ahead of the logo on every page, so someone using a screen reader hears it before anything else.

It works on a phone

The same content, restacked into one column. Nothing is hidden on a small screen.

Part two · this is your part

The editing screen

Everything below happens at the website address with /admin/ on the end, in an ordinary web browser. You need the login that was emailed to you. There are five sections along the top.

SectionWhat it is for
News & noticesMeeting notices and news articles. The one most board members will use
MembersPeople who have signed up for updates. Add someone by hand, see who has confirmed, unsubscribe or remove them, download the list
MessagesWrite an email or a text to members, choose who it goes to, and send it
Firewise groupsThe recognized neighborhoods — their leadership, and when recognition falls due for renewal
BoardWho sits on the board, and who is allowed to sign in here
The four list sections all work the same way — a list, and a form that opens beside it. That is deliberate: learn one and you have learned all four. Every add, edit and delete asks you to confirm first.

Help is built into the screen

The ? Help button at the right-hand end of the section tabs opens help for whichever section you are looking at, and from there you can jump to any other topic — including How the site works, which is worth ten minutes once. You should not need this handbook open while you work.

The main job

Publishing a notice or an article

A meeting notice A box at the very top of the home page, above the headline. For the next meeting, a seminar, a chipper day. One at a time works best.
A news article Appears on the News page. Tick one box and its title also shows on the home page, for the two or three things that matter most right now.
  1. Sign in

    Go to www.austinareafirewise.org/admin/ and click Sign in. The first time, use the temporary password from your email and choose your own — a long phrase is easier to remember than a jumble. You stay signed in for the day.

  2. Write it

    A title, then the text — where, when, what to bring. Leave a blank line between paragraphs. If there is a sign-up page or a map, paste the address and it becomes a button.

  3. Say when it should appear and disappear

    A start time and a stop time, both Central. The item comes and goes on its own — nobody has to remember to take last quarter's meeting down.

  4. Add pictures, if you have them

    Up to three on a news article. Drop a photo in and drag the frame to choose what shows. If it came out on its side, Turn left or Turn right rotates it a quarter turn. Then choose how it sits — across the whole width, or beside the text on the left or the right. Write one sentence describing it: that sentence is what someone who cannot see the picture hears.

  5. Preview, then Publish

    Preview shows the real page with your item in place, exactly as visitors will see it. Use it every time. Then Publish — it is on the site within a minute.

Pictures place themselves down the article in the order you add them — the first above the first paragraph, the next a little further down. On a phone every picture goes full width whatever you chose, because a photo and two words of text side by side is worse than either alone.

A draft can be unfinished

Save as draft takes whatever you have — no title yet, no dates, nothing but a photo you wanted to keep. Come back to it later. Publish is the step that insists on a title, some text, and both dates; nothing reaches the site without them.

The four states an item can be in

Draft Saved, and never visible to the public — not even to someone who guesses the address.
Scheduled Published, but waiting for its start time to arrive.
On the site now Live and visible to everyone.
Ended Past its stop time and gone from the site. Kept for reference — change the dates to bring it back.
Published something wrong? Open it and press Take off the site. It is gone within a minute, and nothing is deleted — it simply becomes a draft again.

Reassurance

What you cannot break

The editing screen was deliberately kept away from the parts people's safety depends on.

  • The emergency information The 911 line and both burn ban notices are part of the site itself. Nothing on the editing screen touches them.
  • The guidance pages Defensible space distances, evacuation advice, home hardening — none of it can be changed from where you publish.
  • Anything by accident Every add, edit and delete asks you to confirm. Drafts are never public, and anything published can be taken down by you in under a minute.
  • The site as a whole The worst thing that can happen is a wrong notice on the home page — and that is yours to fix, without asking anyone.

Updates to members

The sign-up list

The home page has a form where a visitor asks for updates by email, by text, or both. It takes a first and last name, an email address, a mobile number, and — once the board has defined some — which Firewise group they belong to.

Where it stands today: sign-ups are accepted. If our email sending is still going through approval, the record is held and the confirmation follows automatically once it clears — nobody has to come back and enter anything twice. Text messages begin once our phone number clears carrier approval; anyone giving a mobile now is told they will get a confirmation text when it goes live, and nothing is sent until they reply to it.

Three things worth knowing before we start texting

Texts cost money, per person, per message. It is fractions of a cent each, but it is the one part of running this site that scales with how much we use it. The screen where a message is written shows how many segments it will send as, because a slightly-too-long message costs double.

People can leave without asking us. Every email carries a link to a page where someone changes or stops their updates themselves, and every text can be stopped by replying STOP.

We say what we keep and why. The privacy page spells out what is stored about a visitor who only reads the site, and about someone who signs up. It is linked from the footer of every page.

Not your job, usually

The part that still needs a volunteer

The six guidance pages are not edited from the admin screen. They live in files that a volunteer with the right access edits, previews, and publishes. That is deliberate — those pages change rarely, and when they do it is because a number changed at NFPA or the Forest Service, which someone should check against the source first.

Rules that volunteer works to

  • The site speaks as the Alliance, never as an agency. We are an independent volunteer organization — not the City of Austin, AFD, Travis County, or an ESD. Nothing may read as an official notice, and we do not reproduce agency branding.
  • Never publish a fact with a shelf life. A stale "no burn ban in effect" is worse than no notice at all. Where the site does show a live status, it reads it from the agency and hides itself rather than guessing.
  • Emergency instructions belong to the agencies. We point people to them; we do not restate them.
  • Any number that changes gets checked against the source. Zone distances, vent mesh sizes, branch clearances — a typo there is guidance someone may act on.

Know your limits

When to ask for help

  • Try the ? Help button firstEvery section has its own help built into the screen, written for exactly the thing you are looking at.
  • "Signed in, but not on the board list"Your login works but has not been given permission yet. Ask whoever manages the website.
  • A picture will not uploadCheck it is a JPEG, PNG or WebP under 10 MB, and try again in a moment. If it keeps failing, say so.
  • You published it but cannot see itCheck the start time has passed, wait a full minute, then reload the page.
  • Anything about the guidance pagesWording, numbers, the menu, the header or footer — those are files, not admin-screen items.
  • Adding anyone else's logoTrademarks carry usage rules, and placing one prominently can imply an endorsement we do not have.
