# 4 — Editing the site

For board members. How to put a meeting notice or a news article on
**www.austinareafirewise.org** without touching anything technical.

You need the login that was emailed to you. If you never got one, ask whoever
manages the website.

---

## Signing in

1. Go to **www.austinareafirewise.org/admin/**
2. Click **Sign in**
3. Enter your email and password on the page that appears

The first time, you use the temporary password from your email and are asked
to choose your own. Choose something long — a short phrase is easier to
remember than a jumble.

Forgot it? Click **Forgot your password?** on the sign-in page and follow the
email.

You stay signed in for the day. Closing the browser tab signs you out.

---

## What you can put on the site

**A meeting notice** — a box at the very top of the home page, above the big
headline. For the next meeting, a seminar, a chipper day. One at a time is
best.

**A news article** — appears on the **News** page. Tick a box to also show
its title on the home page, under the headline, to draw attention to it.

Both have a **start** and a **stop** time. The item appears at the start and
disappears at the stop, on its own. You do not have to come back and take it
down.

Nothing else on the site can be changed here — the emergency line, the burn
ban banner, and the guidance pages are deliberately not editable.

---

## Adding a meeting notice

1. Click **+ New meeting notice**
2. **Title** — short. "Quarterly meeting — Tuesday 14 October"
3. **Text** — where, when, what to bring. Leave a blank line between paragraphs
4. **Show from** and **Show until** — when it should appear and disappear.
   Times are Central
5. **Link** (optional) — if there is a page to sign up or a map, paste the
   address. It becomes a button. It must start with `https://`
6. Click **Preview** to see it on the real home page. Close the preview
7. Click **Publish**

It is on the site within a minute.

## Adding a news article

The same, with three additions:

- **Show the title in the News section on the home page** — tick this for
  the two or three things that matter most right now. Leave it off for the
  rest; the article is still on the News page
- **Pictures** — see below
- **Text uses Markdown** — leave this off unless you know what Markdown is

Click **+ New news article** to start.

---

## Pictures

Up to three per article.

1. Drop a picture onto the dotted box, or click it and choose one. JPEG, PNG or
   WebP, up to 10 MB — a phone photo is fine
2. A frame appears over the picture. **Drag the frame** to choose what shows.
   **Drag its orange corner** to make the frame bigger or smaller. Every picture
   on the site is the same shape, so the frame keeps that shape
3. **What is in the picture** — required. A short sentence. People who cannot
   see the picture hear this instead: "Volunteers loading branches into the
   chipper at Walnut Creek"
4. **Photo credit** — optional. "Photo: J. Smith"
5. **How it sits in the article** — choose one:
   - **Across the whole width** — a band the width of the text. Good for a
     wide photo, and for the one at the top
   - **Beside the text, on the right** or **on the left** — about a third of
     the width, with the writing flowing around it
6. Click **Add this picture**

Pictures are spread down the article in the order you add them: the first one
above the first paragraph, the next a couple of paragraphs below that. If you
add more pictures than the article has paragraphs, the spare ones go at the
end.

On a phone every picture goes full width whatever you chose, because a picture
and two words of text side by side is worse than either on its own.

If the photo is on its side, **Turn left** or **Turn right** above the frame
rotates it a quarter turn at a time. The frame goes back to the middle after
each turn.

The picture is saved straight away. Under each picture you can change **Where
it sits** at any time, use **Rotate / re-crop** to turn or re-frame one that is
already there — it keeps its description, credit and position — or click
**Remove** to take it out; either way, save or publish afterwards to keep the
change.

Only the cropped part is kept, and it is re-made on your computer before it
is sent, which also removes the location and camera information phones hide
inside photos.

## What kind of notice

A meeting notice carries a small label and a coloured edge saying what it is:

| Kind | Label shown | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meeting | Meeting notice | A meeting, seminar or the AGM |
| Event | Event | A chipper day, work day or open day |
| Urgent | Please read | Something changed, or is time-critical |
| Recognition | Good news | A recognition or a milestone |

You choose the kind and the site chooses the colour. That is on purpose: the
colours keep meaning the same thing across the site, and a notice can never
come out in a colour nobody can read. The label is always shown, so the colour
is never the only thing saying what the notice is.

Use **Please read** sparingly. It is the loudest one and it stops being loud if
everything uses it.

A notice can carry **one** picture, drawn small beside the words. A notice sits
above the headline on the home page, so anything larger pushes the whole page
down — on a phone especially. If you have a poster, make it a news article and
point the notice at it with the link button.

## Help, on the screen

Every section has its own help. The **? Help** button at the right-hand end of
the section tabs opens the help for whichever section you are in, and once it
is open you can switch to any other topic — including **How the site works**,
which is worth ten minutes once.

Beside the Markdown checkbox there is a **What is Markdown?** button. Hover
over it or click it and it shows what to type next to what visitors will see.

---

## Draft, Preview, Publish

Every item is either a **draft** or **published**.

- **Save as draft** keeps your work. Visitors cannot see a draft, ever. A
  draft can be half finished — no title yet, no dates yet, nothing but a
  picture you wanted to keep. Save it and come back to it
- **Publish** is the step that insists on a title, some text and both dates.
  Nothing reaches the site without them
- **Preview** shows the real page with your item in place, exactly as visitors
  will see it. Use it every time
- **Publish** makes it live — within a minute, if the start time has passed;
  otherwise at the start time
- **Take off the site** turns a published item back into a draft. It is not
  deleted
- **Delete** removes it for good. You are asked to confirm

The line at the top of the form tells you which state it is in, and whether
you have unsaved changes.

---

## The list

Items are grouped:

- **On the site now**
- **Scheduled** — published, waiting for their start time
- **Drafts** — visitors cannot see these
- **Ended** — past their stop time. Kept for reference. Change the dates to
  bring one back

Click **Open** to edit any of them.

---

## If something goes wrong

**"Your sign-in has expired"** — sign in again. Your work is saved up to the
last time you clicked Save or Publish.

**"Signed in, but not on the board list"** — your login works but has not
been given permission. Ask whoever manages the website.

**A picture will not upload** — check it is a JPEG, PNG or WebP under 10 MB.
Try again in a moment; if it keeps failing, tell whoever manages the site.

**It is published but I cannot see it** — check the start time has passed,
then wait a full minute and reload the page.

**I published something wrong** — open it and click **Take off the site**.
Gone within a minute.

Nothing you can do here affects the emergency information, the guidance pages,
or anything else on the site. The worst case is a wrong notice, and you can
take that down yourself.
